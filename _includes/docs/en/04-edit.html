{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Editing" title="Edit page" %}

Click a video card on the home screen to open the edit page. The screen consists of the header at the top, the sidebar running vertically along the edge (and the drawer that opens next to it), the video player (with the built-in edit toolbar) in the center, and the timeline at the bottom.

Header (left)
{% include icon.html name="ArrowLeft" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Back button, video title, and 9-digit project ID badge (click to copy)
Metadata
Video length / total frames / fps / resolution / date added
Header (right)
The Open folder / Person gallery / Import into Premiere Pro / Export buttons and the save status. Check the export queue under "Queue" in the menu bar
Save status
Edits are saved automatically about 1 second after each action. "Saving…", "Unsaved", and "Saved" appear in real time, and "Save failed" appears if saving fails. Unsaved changes are also saved when you start an export, leave the edit page, or close the app
Sidebar
Six items: Media / Text / Watermark / Audio / Fade / Transition. Press one to open a drawer next to it, where you can import media and set up text, background music, and more (Sidebar and drawer)
Timeline
Shows the scene strip, the text, clip, and audio lanes, and a row for each detection class. Press the scissors at a join between clips to choose a fade or a transition (Player and timeline / Editing clips / Fade)
Tooltips
Hover over a button, and a tooltip with a description of the function and its shortcut key appears right away (in about 0.15 seconds)
While loading
When you open the edit page, "Loading detection results" appears with the progress until the detection results are ready, and then the player appears with boxes already drawn from the first frame. The first time you open a project detected with an earlier version, "Converting detection results to the new save format" appears (the conversion happens only once)
No detail data
Appears when the detection results file can't be found (for example, because the project folder was moved or deleted). Use "Delete project" on this screen to remove the project from the list
{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Editing" title="Player and timeline" %}

The player overlays detection boxes (BBoxes) in a different color for each class. Drag a box to edit its position and size directly. The player supports stepping frame by frame and seeking. The timeline at the bottom shows the distribution of detections and the scene (cut) strip, and you can click it to jump to any frame.

Detection classes are color-coded: body areas (red) / male face (blue) / female face (pink) / user-created (yellow) / exclusion mask (teal) / mosaic (purple: mosaic already in your footage, only in projects detected with "Mosaic" turned on in the detection options). The label (chip) above each box shows "class name #ID (3-character code) confidence%". Boxes you added manually are labeled "User", exclusion masks are labeled "Exclude", and tracks excluded from mosaic get "[mosaic excluded]". To hide the chips, turn off "Show BBox labels (name, ID, probability)" in "Settings" in the sidebar (the boxes stay visible. Sidebar and drawers).

Mosaic (purple) boxes are polygons (up to 255 points), and as with other polygon boxes, you can move their points to fix the shape ("Range-selecting and bending points" below, Finding existing mosaic and reapplying it).

Playback controls

Play / step frames
Go to start (Home) / Back 1 frame (←) / Play/pause (Space) / Forward 1 frame (→) / Go to end (End). PageUp / PageDown move 10 frames. You can also step frames with the mouse wheel, and Shift+wheel moves 10 frames. If you move forward with Shift+→ (or Shift+wheel up) while a BBox is selected, you advance one frame at a time while keeping that box's shape ("Step frames while carrying over the shape" under "Working with detection boxes" below)
Seek
Drag the slider, or click / drag the timeline. The time and frame number (frame N / M) are shown
Mute (V)
Mutes the preview audio. Doesn't affect export
Toggle overlay (H)
Hides all detection boxes, labels, IDs, and confidence scores so you can check only the video. Your edits don't change
Playback speed
Choose from 0.25x / 0.5x / 1x / 1.5x / 2x / 4x in the toolbar (, to slow down, . to speed up)

Working with detection boxes

Select
Click a box to show a thick border and 8 handles. A dotted line outside the box also shows how far the edge feather reaches (as with mask feather in Premiere Pro, half the setting's width outside the outline. It isn't shown when edge feather is 0, for tracks excluded from mosaic, or when the overlay is hidden (H))
Move / resize
Drag inside a box to move it, or drag a handle to resize it. You can drag even during playback
Delete
Select a box and press Delete, double-click the box, or choose "Delete this BBox" from the right-click menu. Deletion applies only to the box in that frame (boxes with the same ID in other frames remain)
Continuous delete (eraser)
Hold down E and drag on the video with the right mouse button to use the eraser. By default, it deletes the boxes the circle touches from that frame. With the eraser button in the toolbar, you can choose how to erase (Delete touched BBoxes / Delete only touched parts), the shape (Circle / Rounded rectangle), and the size (8-120px). With "Delete only touched parts", the erased area stays cut out of the box's shape (as a polygon), and if a manually added box is split in two, each piece becomes a separate box. If you play or step frames with the wheel while holding the button, you can keep erasing boxes in the frames as they go by (a right-drag without E, or releasing the right button without moving it, opens the usual right-click menu)
Delete by ID (X on the chip)
Press the X at the right end of a detected box's chip to delete that ID's boxes from all frames at once. A confirmation shows the number of frames to be deleted. Chips of manually added boxes don't have an X, but "Delete all of this ID" in the right-click menu does the same thing
Right-click menu
For face boxes, "Change to male face" and "Change to female face" (to correct the detected gender). For all boxes, "Exclude from mosaic" and "Include in mosaic again" (excludes the whole track from mosaic, or includes it again), followed by "Invert (mosaic outside)" and "Invert (blur outside)" ("Invert" below), "Make exclusion mask" ("Change back to mosaic" for an exclusion mask. Changing existing boxes into exclusion masks), "Delete this BBox", and "Delete all of this ID" (for detected boxes, the same as the X on the chip. For manually added continuous BBoxes, single-frame boxes, and exclusion masks, it deletes that box from all frames at once. Any continuous BBoxes merged into a detected ID are deleted along with it, and the confirmation shows how many. Either way, one Ctrl+Z undoes it). When a continuous BBox has a motion path, "Clear motion path". When there are IDs that can be merged, "Merge IDs…", and for merged IDs, "Unmerge" ("Merging IDs" below). With "Change shape", choose a scope (this BBox / all of this ID / all) and change the shape to rectangle / ellipse / polygon. "Reset shape to default" restores the original shape. "Connect from here", and when a connection source is chosen, "Add as waypoint" and "Connect to here". For keyframes and connecting boxes, see Tracking and keyframes
Invert (apply outside)
Right-click a box and choose "Invert (mosaic outside)" or "Invert (blur outside)" to turn on invert for that ID. For an inverted box, the menu item changes to "Turn off invert (apply inside)", which restores it. Not available for exclusion masks. For where it applies and how to switch it on the "By ID" tab, see "Invert (apply outside)" in the Mosaic settings panel
Merging IDs
If the same subject has been split into different IDs, right-click the box you want to keep and choose "Merge IDs…". A list of IDs that can be merged ("IDs to merge into #XXX") opens, nearest first. Select the ID you want to combine, and it is merged into the ID you right-clicked. Each row in the list shows the #ID and class name, the segment (frames and time), and how many frames later or earlier it is (or "Adjacent"), and the selected box appears at the top marked "Selected". You can also select with ↑ / ↓ and Enter, and close the list with Esc. When there are more than 8, use "N more IDs" to show the rest. Right-clicking an ID row in the timeline below opens the same list. Once merged, they are treated as a single ID (in timeline rows, per-ID mosaic settings, and export), and any frames between them without boxes are interpolated automatically. You can merge IDs of the same class that don't appear at the same time (subjects that appear at the same time are different subjects). When no IDs can be merged, the list shows "No IDs can be merged"
Merging continuous BBoxes
You can also merge continuous BBoxes (∞) with each other, or a continuous BBox with a detected ID. If you right-click a continuous BBox and choose a detected ID, the detected ID is the one kept (that row in the list shows "The detected ID becomes the main one"). When two continuous BBoxes overlap in time, the one that starts first is cut off just before the other one starts (unmerging restores it). Exclusion masks can't be merged
Undoing and unmerging
Right after merging, one Ctrl+Z takes you back to before the merge. To undo a merge later, right-click the merged box and choose "Unmerge (N IDs)", or right-click the ID row in the timeline and choose "Unmerge (N IDs)" at the end of the list
Range-selecting and bending points (Shift+left-drag)
Select a polygon box, then hold down Shift and left-drag around points to select them together (they are grouped into a connected segment. If you enclose just one point, that point is selected. A short tap or Esc clears the selection). Drag a point inside the selected segment to bend it with both ends of the segment fixed, so the middle moves the most. If you enclose all the points, the shape moves without changing. If you drag a point at an end of the segment, only that point moves. While multiple points are selected, you can't delete points with Delete (select and delete them one at a time)
Mosaic exclusion notice
When any track is excluded, "N tracks excluded from mosaic" is always shown. "Apply mosaic to all again" includes them all again at once. Excluded areas don't get mosaic even when you export
Preview margin and outside the video
In the preview, there is a margin around the video on all sides (about 15% on each side, dark gray), and a thin line marks the edge of the video. You can move or enlarge a box so that it extends into the margin, so even when a person is leaving the frame, you can place an ellipse or other shape without squashing it. The part that extends beyond the video isn't exported, but inside the video, mosaic is applied in that shape right up to the edge. A new box must overlap the video, and dragging only in the margin doesn't draw one
Detection boxes cut off at the edge
A detection box cut off at the edge of the frame is automatically extended beyond the edge of the frame, based on the box size in the frames before and after it touches the edge. Mosaic is drawn only inside the frame, but its shape (such as an ellipse) is based on the extended box, so even when a face is leaving the frame, the mosaic shape isn't squashed at the edge. This applies to both the preview and the export. This automatic extension is available for videos detected with version 2.8.1 or later
Draw a box (left-drag)
Left-drag on an empty area of the video to draw a box in the shape selected in the toolbar's shape picker (rectangle / ellipse / polygon). For a polygon, click to place points, and close it by clicking the starting point, pressing Enter, or double-clicking. By default, a drawn box is a continuous BBox that continues into later frames. In the shape picker menu, you can change "Drawn BBox" to "This frame only", and "Drawn BBox type" to "Exclusion mask". Dragging only in the margin doesn't draw a box (draw it so it overlaps the video). To move the view while zoomed in, use Ctrl+left-drag (middle-button drag also works)
How manually added boxes appear
The chip of a manually added box shows "User", and its ID is a 3-character code, the same as for detected boxes (for example, #K7Q). In the timeline, it appears in the "User" row
Carrying over mosaic settings
When you add a box on top of a detected box, or near one (with a gap of no more than 5% of the shorter side of the frame), the mosaic settings in effect for that detected box (type, blur, opacity, block size) are copied to it. The block size is fixed so that it looks the same as that detected box. You can change the copied settings at any time on the "By ID" tab of Mosaic settings
Step frames while carrying over the shape (Shift+→)
Click a box to select it, then hold down Shift and press → (or scroll the wheel up). You advance one frame at a time with the position, size, and shape the box had when you started holding Shift, and that box is overwritten in each frame you advance to. The chip of the box being carried over shows "[carrying over]". Everything until you release Shift counts as one action, and one Ctrl+Z undoes it all. Frames where that box doesn't exist are skipped, and if not a single frame could be written, "This BBox isn't in the frames you moved to, so its shape couldn't be carried over." appears. Shift+← goes back one frame without carrying over the shape. The shape isn't carried over during playback with the play button, when you move 10 frames with PageDown, for single-frame manual boxes, or in segments of imported media
Locking carry-over (Shift twice)
Select a box and press Shift twice quickly to make carry-over "locked", so it continues even after you release Shift. The chip shows "[carry-over: locked]". While locked, → or wheel up without Shift also advances while carrying over the shape (PageDown and the play button don't carry it over). Press Shift twice quickly again, or press Esc, to unlock. If you select a different box while locked, that box's shape is carried over from the next time you advance. On systems where software such as a Japanese input method (IME) uses a single press of Shift, locking may not work

Selecting, adding, and erasing detection boxes work only when the playhead is in a segment of the original video (the detection source). Segments of imported media aren't subject to AI detection, so they have no boxes.

Timeline

From top to bottom, the timeline shows the toolbar, the ruler, the scene (cut) strip, the text, clip, and audio lanes (Editing clips), and a row for each detection class. Each class row shows bands for the segments that have detections. Manually added boxes appear in the "User" row, and exclusion masks in the "Exclude" row. Use {% include icon.html name="ChevronDown" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} at the start of a row to expand it into a row for each tracking ID. Right-click an ID row to open the list of IDs that can be merged into that ID ("Merging IDs" above. For merged IDs, "Unmerge" appears at the end of the list). Right-clicking doesn't move the playhead to that position. Tracks excluded from mosaic are shown faded. The timeline is laid out in the time of the exported video, so when you trim or move a clip, the detection bands move with it.

Zoom the timeline up to 16x with Ctrl+wheel or the zoom buttons at the left end of the toolbar. For changing the height and collapsing the timeline, see Editing clips.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Editing" title="Zoom and pan" %}

To fit boxes precisely around small subjects, you can zoom the player's video up to 8x. While zoomed in, you can still move, resize, add, and delete boxes as usual.

Zoom in / out
Ctrl+wheel (zooms around the cursor position), the + / - keys, or the {% include icon.html name="Maximize2" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} buttons at the top right of the player. The current zoom level is shown between the buttons
Pan (move the view)
Ctrl+left-drag, or drag with the middle button (wheel). Doesn't work when you aren't zoomed in (100%). Left-dragging on an empty area draws a box
Reset
Press the 0 key or the "Reset zoom" button to return to 100% and center the view
{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Editing" title="Adding detection boxes (1f and ∞)" %}

Where nothing was detected, you can add boxes manually with the "1f" (a square with a + and 1f) and "∞" (a square with ∞) buttons in the toolbar. An added box is placed in the center of the current frame as the "User" class (yellow), so drag it to adjust its position and size. Mosaic settings (including per-ID settings) apply to manual boxes as well.

1f (1)
Places a box in this frame only. The frames before and after aren't affected. Useful for patching a "Gap" (a few frames where detection drops out)
∞ continuous BBox (2)
The box continues in the same position in every frame from the one where you placed it onward. If you don't want it to continue to the end of the video, delete the box at the frame where you want it to end (the range up to just before the deleted frame becomes its confirmed range)
Moving a continuous BBox
Drag the ∞ box during playback to record the movement as a motion path, so the box follows the subject as it moves. To delete a recorded path, use "Clear motion path" in the right-click menu. To have a box follow the subject automatically, use tracking
Undo
If the last edit was merging IDs, deleting all, applying a shape to a range, or changing a class into exclusion masks, Ctrl+Z undoes only that (Working with detection boxes). Otherwise, right after you end an ∞ box by deleting it, it first undoes the confirmed end (shown as "Unfreeze" in the shortcut legend); if there is none, it reverts the edits in the current frame, the same as "Reset current frame"

Exclusion mask (areas without mosaic)

To keep mosaic off just part of the area it covers (for example, to show only the hands next to a face), place an exclusion mask. No mosaic is applied inside an exclusion mask, and that area is shown and exported as the original video. Exclusion masks don't follow the subject, so for a moving subject, drag the mask into position.

1f exclusion mask (3)
Places it in this frame only
∞ continuous exclusion mask (4)
Continues it into the frames after the one where you placed it (end it the same way as a continuous BBox: delete it at the frame where you want it to end)
Shape and drawing
Placed in the shape selected in the toolbar's shape picker (rectangle / ellipse / polygon). Set "Drawn BBox type" in the shape picker menu to "Exclusion mask" to draw exclusion masks with a left-drag
Display
The box and chip are teal and labeled "Exclude", and the mask appears in the "Exclude" row of the timeline. In the mosaic preview (M), you can see how the mosaic is cut out
Edge feather
The edge of an exclusion mask is blended in with the same edge feather setting as manual boxes. Size (box expansion) has no effect
Premiere Pro
Exclusion masks aren't passed to Premiere Pro integration

Changing existing boxes into exclusion masks

You can also change detected boxes and manually added boxes into exclusion masks later. Inside a box changed into an exclusion mask, other overlapping mosaics aren't applied either.

Changing one at a time
Right-click a box and select "Make exclusion mask". For a detected box, all frames of that ID (including merged IDs) become an exclusion mask. For a manually added box, that box becomes an exclusion mask. To change it back, right-click the exclusion mask and select "Change back to mosaic"
Changing a whole class at once
In the "By class" tab of Mosaic settings (G), choose a class and press "Make all of this class exclusion masks". A confirmation showing the number of IDs appears. It applies to the detected IDs of that class that exist at that time (for "Face", both male and female faces; manually added boxes and IDs added later aren't included). "Change all of this class back to mosaic" changes them back all at once. Either way, one Ctrl+Z undoes it
Display
A box changed into an exclusion mask is shown in teal as "Exclude", like other exclusion masks, and appears in the "Exclude" row of the timeline. IDs changed into exclusion masks can't be merged. If you merge IDs and then change the ID into an exclusion mask, the IDs separated by "Unmerge" go back to mosaic
Premiere Pro and earlier versions
Changes into exclusion masks aren't passed to Premiere Pro integration. If you open and save the project in a version of Deepmosaic earlier than 2.18, detected boxes changed into exclusion masks go back to mosaic (they go back to being hidden, so no area is left uncovered)

Adding, moving, and deleting boxes are all saved automatically and reflected in the export. Only the detection results and the edge feather value are passed to Premiere Pro integration. Box edits on this screen (moving, changing shapes, deleting, exclusion masks, and so on) aren't passed.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Editing" title="Tracking and keyframes" %}

"Tracking" automatically fits an ∞ continuous BBox you placed by hand to the subject's movement as the video plays. Turn it on or off with the button in the control row. "Keyframes" use the frames you corrected by hand as reference points for the movement, and automatically connect the frames between one keyframe and the next.

Tracking

  1. Place a box. Place or draw an ∞ continuous BBox (2) and fit it to the subject (Adding detection boxes)
  2. Turn on tracking mode. Press the tracking button ({% include icon.html name="Crosshair" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %}) in the control row, or press X
  3. Move forward. Play the video, press →, or scroll the wheel up, and the box moves to follow the subject
Tracking mode (X)
While it's on, moving forward (playback / → / wheel up) tracks the subject. Moving back, such as with ←, doesn't track. Press it again to turn it off
Boxes that are tracked
All ∞ continuous BBoxes visible in the current frame are tracked. You don't need to select them. Multiple boxes can be tracked at the same time, with no limit on the number. The more boxes there are, the slower tracking becomes, and playback waits for tracking accordingly. 1f boxes and exclusion masks aren't tracked ("Exclusion masks can't be tracked.")
Playback speed
While tracking, the video plays at 1x speed or slower. If tracking can't keep up, playback pauses until it catches up
When it stops
When the subject is lost, leaves the frame, or the scene changes, tracking stops and the reason appears (for example, "Stopped tracking because the target was lost here."). In that case, the ∞ box ends at the last frame that could be tracked and doesn't continue past it. When tracking reaches the end of the video, "Tracked to the end of the video." appears
Undo (Ctrl+Z)
The result of one tracking run, even when multiple boxes were tracked at the same time, can be undone all at once with one Ctrl+Z, back to before tracking started (the end of each box is restored too)
Boxes already tracked
A box that has been tracked once isn't tracked again when you play the video again. If you correct a box by hand with tracking mode on, it is tracked again from the corrected frame onward (up to just before the next frame you corrected by hand). To track again from the beginning, delete the box and place it again
Tracking type
"High-accuracy tracking" is selected automatically on PCs with a GPU, and "Standard tracking" on other PCs. Standard tracking is used while detection is running. After tracking starts, hover over the tracking button to see which tracking is in use, such as "In use: High-accuracy tracking (GPU)"
Newer detection engine required
If "Tracking needs a newer detection engine." appears, apply the update

Keyframes

When you correct an ∞ continuous BBox
For an ∞ box that hasn't been tracked, the frame you corrected by hand becomes a "keyframe", and the frames between it and the previous keyframe are connected smoothly and automatically. If you correct a tracked ∞ box with tracking mode off, only the corrected frame changes
Keyframe (F)
Makes the selected box's current position a keyframe without moving it
When you correct an AI box
Only the corrected frame changes

Connecting

Use boxes in separate frames as points to create one ∞ continuous BBox that moves between them following the subject's movement. Besides the start and the end, you can place any number of points along the way (waypoints).

  1. Place the start point. Select a box at the starting frame and press Y (or select "Connect from here" in the box's right-click menu). The bottom left of the player shows something like "Connect from: #K7Q @ 3" with a guide to the next step
  2. Place waypoints if needed. Move to a frame along the way, select a box that fits the subject, and press Shift+Y (or select "Add as waypoint" in the right-click menu). You can place as many as you like. Placing one again on the same frame replaces it ("Replaced the point on the same frame.")
  3. Connect at the end point. Select a box at the ending frame and press Y (or select "Connect to here" in the right-click menu). The points are connected right away with smooth interpolation, and a message such as "Connected the BBoxes (#K7Q → #K7Q, 31 frames)" appears. When you placed waypoints, their number is also shown
  4. Tracking runs in order. The connected section goes into the "Tracking queue", and the boxes between the points are corrected to follow the subject's movement ("Tracking queue" below). You don't have to wait for it to finish; you can go on to the next connection or other edits
Boxes you can connect
The boxes used as points can be AI boxes or manually added boxes. You can connect boxes in different frames within the same scene (the same applies to waypoints)
When the subject can't be followed
When tracking loses the subject, the part that could be followed is applied, and the rest stays smoothly interpolated (the row in the tracking queue shows something like "Lost the target at f120"). When the start point is an exclusion mask, or when you connect boxes in adjacent frames, the connection is made with interpolation only and doesn't go into the tracking queue. When tracking can't be used, the connection is also made with interpolation only, and "Couldn't follow the subject, so the BBoxes were connected with smooth interpolation." appears
Using a continuous BBox as a point
When the box at the start point or a waypoint is an ∞ continuous BBox, it changes to a box on that frame only when you use it as a point (if the box was placed on that frame, it becomes a single-frame box; if it continues from an earlier frame, it ends just before this frame and a single-frame box is created on this frame). When the box at the end point is an ∞ continuous BBox, that box ends just before the point (it's removed if it was placed on that frame) and is replaced by the connected box
The original boxes
The original AI box isn't removed, and in the frames in between it overlaps the connected box. Single-frame boxes used as points are removed. The connected box ends at the frame of the end point
Correcting the middle
A box connected by tracking is treated as an already tracked box. If you correct a frame in the middle with tracking mode (X) on, it is tracked again from there up to just before the next point. With it off, only the corrected frame changes. For a box connected entirely with smooth interpolation, if you correct it at a frame in the middle while paused, that frame becomes a keyframe and the frames before and after it are reconnected smoothly ("Keyframes" above)
Undo (Ctrl+Z)
Right after connecting, one Ctrl+Z takes you back to before connecting (if you used a continuous BBox as a point, it goes back to the original continuous BBox. The same applies after tracking in the tracking queue finishes, as long as you haven't made other edits). After you correct the middle, Ctrl+Z doesn't take you back to before connecting, so to start over, delete the box with "Delete all of this ID" in its right-click menu
Stop (Esc)
To stop after placing points, press Esc. If boxes at the points were changed to single-frame boxes, they stay that way (pressing Ctrl+Z right after Esc restores all of them to the original continuous BBoxes; this isn't possible if you make another edit in between)
Correcting the section before tracking
If you correct a box in the section by hand while it's waiting in the tracking queue or being tracked, that connection is removed from the queue and the boxes stay interpolated ("Tracking was canceled because the box in this segment was edited by hand. The interpolated box is kept.")
When boxes can't be connected
In these cases the boxes aren't connected and the reason appears: boxes in the same frame ("Can't connect: it's the same frame."), boxes in different scenes ("Can't connect: BBoxes in different scenes can't be connected."), an exclusion mask in between ("Can't connect: there's an exclusion mask in between."), and a BBox paired with an exclusion mask ("Can't connect: a BBox and an exclusion mask are different types.")

Tracking queue

Tracking for connected sections goes into the "Tracking queue" and runs one job at a time, in order. On PCs with a GPU, contour refinement (like mosaic refinement, it fits the box to the shape of the object) also runs after tracking. When a job finishes, the interpolated boxes between the points are replaced with the result of tracking (and refinement). Boxes on the frames of the points you placed by hand don't change.

Panel and indicators
When there are connected sections, a small indicator such as "Tracking 45% · 2 waiting" (with a progress bar) appears above the player. Press it to open the "Tracking queue" panel, and press "—" on the panel to go back to the small indicator. You can drag the panel and the small indicator, and their position and open state carry over the next time you open the edit page. The status bar at the bottom of the screen also shows the progress, such as "● Tracking 45% (3 jobs)", and pressing it opens or closes the panel (it shows "Tracking: nothing queued" when there's nothing, and "● Tracking error" when, for example, a section lost the target)
Reading the panel
From the top: the running job (state "Tracking", "Refining outline", or "Paused", the ID, the frames of points A and B, the progress %, the speed, and the estimated time left), the "Queued · N" list, and the "Finished · N" list. The bottom of the panel shows the estimated time left for everything, such as "About 2 min 10 s left in total", and "All jobs processed" when everything is done
Pause / cancel
Press "Pause" on the running row to stop it, and "Resume" to continue from where it stopped. If you stop the job with ✕ ("Cancel"), the boxes stay interpolated
Reorder / remove
↑ ("Move up") on a queued row swaps it with the one before it. If you remove it with ✕ ("Remove from queue"), the boxes in that section stay interpolated
Review / retry
Press "Review" on a finished row to move to the start of that section and select the resulting box. A row where the target was lost is shown in red, such as "Lost the target at f120". The part followed before the target was lost is applied, and the rest stays interpolated. "Retry" puts it back in the queue. When tracking stops partway, "Tracking could not be completed" appears. While detection is running, contour refinement isn't done and only the tracking result is applied ("Detection is running, so only the tracking result was applied without refining the outline.")

The tracking queue works only while the edit page is open. If you leave the edit page, export, or send to Premiere Pro while jobs are unfinished, a confirmation "Tracking is not finished" appears. If you continue, the unfinished sections stay interpolated

The sections in the queue are shown as bands above the seek bar (progress for the running job, green when finished). You can hide them with "Show tracking segments on the seek bar" in "Settings" in the sidebar

Fixing a range of frames at once

When you correct an AI box by hand, only that frame changes. To fix a range of several seconds to several minutes at once, use the following actions depending on the situation.

The subject moves
Use the boxes at the start and the end (and along the way) as points and connect them by tracking (Y / Shift+Y) ("Connecting" above). To fix them by hand, pause with tracking mode off, place an ∞ continuous BBox (2) at the start of the range and fit it to the subject, then correct its position and size at frames in the middle and at the end. The frames between the corrected frames are connected smoothly and automatically ("Keyframes" above). When the movement is complex, let tracking (X) follow the subject and correct only the frames where the box drifts ("Tracking" above)
The subject barely moves
Select the corrected box, press Shift twice quickly, and advance with →. The same position, size, and shape are written to the frames ahead (Locking carry-over). Press Esc to unlock
Enlarging or shrinking all boxes in a range
In the "By time" tab of Mosaic settings (G), press "Add range", set the start and end to the range, and change "Size (box expansion)". It applies to all boxes in the range (including manually added boxes). To change them by scene, choose that scene in "Target scene:" in the "By scene" tab (press Ctrl+K at the start and the end of the range to split it. Scenes). To change only a specific subject, use the "By ID" tab (it applies to all frames of that ID)

Keep the following in mind.

  • Manually added boxes overlap AI boxes, and the AI boxes remain. In ranges where an AI box is too large or off, erase it continuously with the eraser (play while holding E+right-drag), or use "Exclude from mosaic" in the box's right-click menu to exclude that whole ID from mosaic (Working with detection boxes)
  • Interpolation between keyframes works only when you correct a box while paused with tracking mode off. It doesn't happen while tracking mode is on or when you drag during playback (which records a motion path)
  • An ∞ continuous BBox keeps appearing in the same position in the frames ahead until you delete it (it doesn't stop when the scene changes). Select the box at the frame where you want it to end and press Delete, or select "Delete this BBox" in the right-click menu, and it's removed from that frame onward
  • Connecting works only between boxes within the same scene
  • Manually added boxes (∞ continuous BBoxes and connected boxes) keep the size from when they were created as a "By ID" setting. Values you set after that in "All", "By scene", "By time", and so on also apply to manually added boxes (the setting changed last takes priority. Mosaic settings panel)
  • To check the area the mosaic covers, switch the preview to the mosaic preview (M)

Type

Size (box expansion)
Enlarges or shrinks the detection box from its center to adjust the mosaic area (0.50x to 2.00x). Default: 1.05x. Make it larger if you want to leave some margin

How strongly the mosaic/blur is blended in (0-100%). At the default of 100%, the subject is fully covered. The lower the value, the less it hides, so normally leave it at 100%

Feathers the edges of the mosaic area to blend them with the background (0-64px). Default: 6px. The "Set pixel size" button opens the settings screen. The current setting is shown below the button (for example, Fixed: 53 blocks across, By size range: 42-96 blocks across, or Proportional by split count /16). Shown only when the type is "Mosaic". On the "By ID", "By scene", and "By time" tabs, you can press it after choosing a target. Reading the settings screen
The preview and the graph are on the left. Drag the ellipse box in the preview to move it, or use its bottom-right handle to resize it, and mosaic is applied inside the box with the actual blocks. For the preview image, choose a person or a test pattern, or replace it with your own image. The graph shows the relationship between the target size (horizontal axis, as a proportion of the frame width) and the number of blocks (vertical axis, finer toward the top), and you can change it by dragging the lines and handles. The result at the top right shows the number of blocks for the current box (for example, 53 blocks across) and what determined it (range, split count, lower limit, upper limit, and so on). The minimum mosaic under the self-regulation standard (long side / 100 when the long side is 400px or more, minimum 4px) always takes precedence, so you can't choose a number of blocks finer than that
Fixed
The setting used when "Automatic pixel size" is off. Uses the same block size for all frames and all targets. Specify it with "Blocks across the width" (the default is 53 blocks across. Fewer blocks are coarser, and more blocks are finer)
By size range
Choose this with "Automatic pixel size" on. Sets the number of blocks across the width for each range of target size (the representative box size for each combination of cut and ID, expressed as a proportion of the frame width). You can have 1 to 6 ranges, and you can add, delete, or reset them to the default. Within the same cut and the same ID, the block size stays constant and doesn't flicker from frame to frame
Proportional by split count
Choose this with "Automatic pixel size" on. Divides the target size by "Fineness (split count across the target)" (8-64, default 16) and uses the result as the length of one side of a block. The larger the target appears, the larger the blocks, and the higher the value, the finer the mosaic. "Coarsest block" (N or more blocks across) sets a limit on how coarse it can get (you can also choose no limit). Within the same cut and the same ID, the block size stays constant
Earlier projects
Earlier settings are converted to the new settings when you open the project. Automatic (target-based) and fixed (resolution-based) settings look the same as before. Manual (split count) settings are converted to "Proportional by split count", which splits based on the target size rather than the box width, so the block size may change even with the same split count (coarser for tall targets and finer for wide targets). When a setting is converted, the settings screen shows "Converted your previous manual setting (split count N)"

Takes each block's color from a single sample point instead of the area average (equivalent to Sharp Colors in Premiere Pro; off by default)

  2. Set the "Start" and "End". Type them in the min:sec:frame format (e.g. 1:05:12), or move the playhead and press "Use current position"
  3. Change the type, size, opacity, and so on, the same way as on the other tabs

Choose a range in "Range". "Delete this range" deletes it. If the start is after the end, "Start must be before end" appears

Ranges set by time are shown as bands below the scene bands on the timeline. Click a band to open the "By time" tab with that range selected. The bands of ranges that don't take effect (for example, after "All" was changed later. "Setting priority" below) are shown faded

Ranges are kept in the time of the original video, so even if you split, trim, or move clips, they apply to the same part of the original video (when a clip uses the same part twice, they apply in both places). Which boxes
It applies to both detected boxes and manually added boxes in the range (except IDs with "Invert (apply outside)" turned on)

Setting priority

When settings from "All", "By class", "By ID", "By scene", and "By time" overlap on the same box in the same frame, the setting changed last takes priority, and all items of that setting are used as they are (items aren't mixed).

  • A setting counts as "changed" when you actually change a value in the panel (including resetting it to the default with {% include icon.html name="RotateCcw" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %}) and when you add or change a range on the By time tab. Choosing the same value again doesn't change the order
  • If you change "All" later, the By class, By ID, By scene, and By time settings made before it no longer take effect, and all items take the values of "All". Even if you change the value back, the order isn't restored
  • When you open a setting that doesn't take effect on its tab, a message such as "This setting has no effect because "All" was changed later" appears. Press "Prioritize this setting" to make that setting the one changed last without changing its values, so it takes effect again
  • When a change makes other settings stop taking effect, a notification such as "2 other settings no longer take effect after this change" appears. Press "Undo" in the notification to go back to the values and order before that change (only the most recent change. If you made another edit in between, "Couldn't undo (another edit was made after it)" appears)
  • "By ID" settings added automatically when you add or paste a box or connect boxes (Carrying over mosaic settings) don't count as "changed". Right after the box is created, it looks the same as before, and settings changed after that take priority
  • For IDs with "Invert (apply outside)" turned on, the settings of that ID are used regardless of the order. The per-ID settings of boxes for existing mosaic (including the block settings added by detection) are also used regardless of the order. Mosaic exclusion, exclusion masks, and the confidence threshold also apply regardless of the order
  • Projects created before 2.18 look the same as before when you open them (settings without a recorded order take priority in the previous order: By ID, By class, By scene, All)
  • If you open a project in a version of Deepmosaic earlier than 2.18, the By time settings and the order are ignored, and the previous order is used (if you save it in that version, the By time settings and the order are lost)
  • By time settings aren't passed to Premiere Pro integration

{% include icon.html name="Images" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Media (A)
Import video, audio, and image media and add it to the timeline (Media)
{% include icon.html name="Type" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Text
Burn text into the video (Text)
{% include icon.html name="Stamp" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Watermark
Overlay an image such as a logo on the entire video (Watermark)
{% include icon.html name="Music" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Audio
Adjust the original video's volume and audio tracks such as background music (Audio and background music)
{% include icon.html name="Blend" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Fade
Set the fade length in seconds for the start and end of the whole exported video, and for the start and end of the selected clip (Fade)
{% include icon.html name="ArrowLeftRight" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Transition
Set the type and length of the switching effect added at a join between clips. The sidebar label is also "Transition" (Transition)
T key
Opens or closes the drawer of the item you opened last, from Text through Transition ("Watermark" the first time)
{% include icon.html name="Settings" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} Settings
The gear at the bottom of the sidebar opens "Settings", where you choose the default values for editing: the persistence of BBoxes you draw (this and later frames / this frame only), their shape (rectangle / ellipse / polygon) and type (mosaic / exclusion mask), whether the HUD (boxes and chips) is shown, whether the box chips are shown ("Show BBox labels (name, ID, probability)"; the boxes stay visible when it's off), the eraser's erase mode, shape, and size, whether tracking mode is on when you open the edit page, and whether the sections in the tracking queue are shown on the seek bar ("Show tracking segments on the seek bar"). "Settings" opens without darkening the screen, and you can drag its header to move it, so you can change settings while looking at the preview (it appears in the center each time you open it). Changed values are saved immediately and become the defaults when you start the app (values you switch in the toolbar while editing are not saved). "Reset to default" restores the factory values
Position
In "Settings", "Position" (Left / Right) under "Display" chooses whether the sidebar and drawer are on the left or the right side of the screen. The default is left, and "Reset to default" puts them back on the left
Drawer width
Drag the edge of the drawer on the player side to change its width between 240 and 520px. You can also select the edge and press the ← / → keys to adjust it in 16px steps. Double-click to return to the default 300px

The drawer width and its left or right position carry over the next time you open the edit page.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Editing clips" %}

The "Clip lane" of the timeline shows the sequence of the exported video as bars (clips). At first there is a single clip of the original video (the detection source). You can trim, split, and delete to cut unwanted parts, or add imported media before or after it. The text lane is above the clip lane and the audio lane is below it, and all lanes are laid out on the exported video's time.

Your edits show up in the preview as is and are included when you export. Mosaic is applied to the sections from the original video.

Select
Click a bar to select it. A delete button (X) appears at the top right of the bar
Trim
Drag the left or right edge of a bar to trim the start or end of the clip (minimum 1 frame). The edge snaps when it gets close to a scene boundary or the playhead. Imported videos can be extended up to the length of the media, and image clips to any length
Split (Ctrl+K / Ctrl+Shift+K)
Use the {% include icon.html name="Scissors" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} button to the right of the timeline zoom buttons, or Ctrl+K (Ctrl+Shift+K when the timeline has focus), to split the clip in two at the playhead and split the scene at the same time. The clip bar immediately becomes two, a cut mark (diagonal lines and scissors) appears at the join, and the scene strip gets a new number. The second half is selected, so you can go on to trim or delete it. A single "Undo" restores both the clip and the scene
Delete
Select a clip and press the delete button (X) on the bar, press the Backspace / Delete key, or choose "Delete" from the right-click menu. Later clips move forward to close the gap (ripple delete)
Reorder and move
Grab a bar and drag it left or right to reorder it according to where you drop it. It snaps to the edges of other clips, scene boundaries, and the playhead. You can also move it past the current end. Use Ctrl+← / Ctrl+→ to move the selected clip 1 frame at a time
Gap
If you place a clip away from the previous clip, the space between them becomes a gap (black screen, no sound), and a bar with diagonal lines shows "Gap N frames". The exported video also has a black screen with no sound there. To close a gap, drag the clip to the edge of the previous clip (it snaps to the edge)
Join
A {% include icon.html name="Scissors" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} badge (scissors) appears at each join between clips. Press the badge to open a small menu where you choose "Fade" or "Transition". When you choose one, the corresponding tab in the sidebar (Fade / Transition) opens with that join in focus. Set the length in seconds and the type there
Filmstrip
Video clip bars show thumbnails of their content (filmstrip). Image clips show the image itself. Until the thumbnails finish loading, the bars are shown in a plain color

Right-click menu and clipboard

Right-click a clip, text, or audio track bar to show a menu with "Duplicate", "Copy", "Paste", and "Delete". If you right-click an empty area of a lane, only "Paste" appears. The menu items and the shortcuts do the same thing.

Duplicate (Ctrl+D)
Duplicates a clip right after it, text right after its bar, and an audio track at the same position
Copy / Paste (Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V)
Pastes the copied element. Text and audio tracks go at the playhead, and clips go right after the selected clip (or, if no clip is selected, the clip at the playhead). The clipboard holds only the one element you copied last, and it is separate from your computer's clipboard (it works only within the edit page)
Delete (Delete)
Deletes the selected element. If you last worked in the player, Delete deletes a detection box instead (Shortcuts)

Undo and history

Timeline edits (clips, text, audio, watermark, fade, transition, and adding media) are recorded in a history separate from detection box edits. Use "Undo" (Ctrl+Shift+Z) / "Redo" (Ctrl+Shift+Y) to the right of the timeline zoom buttons to go one step at a time, or choose an entry in the list from the {% include icon.html name="History" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} history button to undo everything back to that operation at once. The list shows operation names such as "Split", "Trim", "Move", and "Add text" with their times, newest first, and keeps up to 100 entries. The history is cleared when you reopen the video.

Timeline height and collapsing

Changing the height
Drag the top edge of the timeline to change its height between 220 and 720px (at least 200px always remains for the player). You can also select the top edge and press the ↑ / ↓ keys to adjust it in 16px steps. Double-click to return to the default 400px
Collapsing (Ctrl+Shift+T)
The {% include icon.html name="ChevronDown" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} button at the right end of the toolbar collapses the timeline to just the toolbar row, so the player can use more of the screen. Press it again to expand the timeline

The height and the collapsed state carry over the next time you open the edit page.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Media (importing assets)" %}

In the media panel, which you open with "Media" (A) in the sidebar, import the video, audio, and image media you want to use on the timeline. Imported media is copied into the project folder, so you can keep using it even if you move the original file (it's also included in the zip when you export the project).

Importing
Use "Import media" to choose the type (video / audio / image) and select a file, or drag and drop files from File Explorer onto the edit page
Supported formats and limits
Video: the same formats as in Loading videos (up to 2GB per file) / Audio: MP3, WAV, AAC, M4A, FLAC, OGG, Opus (up to 200MB) / Image: PNG, JPEG, WebP, BMP (up to 50MB)
Adding to the timeline
"Add to timeline" on a media row places a video at the end as a clip, and audio at the playhead as an audio track. You can also drag media onto a lane to place it wherever you like (videos and images on the clip lane, audio on the audio lane)
Images
"Add to timeline" places an image as a 5-second image clip, and you can drag the edge of the bar to stretch or shrink it to any length. Choose "Set as watermark" to use the image as a logo over the entire video (Watermark)
Video audio
In sections where an imported video is placed as a clip, the export doesn't include that video's audio, and those sections are silent (audio tracks such as background music still play). To use the audio of an imported video, drop the same media onto the audio lane as well
Deleting media
The delete button on a row deletes the media from the project. The file itself is also deleted, so you can't undo this (timeline undo doesn't restore it either). Media used on the timeline can't be deleted

Imported media isn't covered by AI detection. Mosaic isn't applied to media sections, and you can't add detection boxes or use the eraser there. For footage that needs mosaic, first load it as a video, run detection, export it, and then import the result.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Text" %}

Use "Text" in the sidebar to burn text into the video. You can add as many text items as you like and set the display range, position, and format of each. The display range is specified with frame numbers of the exported video.

Adding
"Add text" adds a text item reading "Text" at the bottom center, shown throughout the video. Select it from the list to edit it
Display range
Enter the start and end frame numbers. "Set to playhead" enters the playhead position. Select the "Show until the end" check box to show the text until the end of the video
Position
Drag the text in the preview to move it freely to any position. To set it numerically, use "Position X / Y (%)" (the position from the top left). Choosing a "Position preset" (9 positions from top left to bottom right) aligns the text with a 2% margin from the edge
Size
Specify the text height as a percentage of the video height (2 to 50%, default 6%)
Font and bold
There are 5 fonts: Gothic (default) / Yu Gothic / Mincho / MS Gothic / BIZ UDGothic. You can also make the text "Bold". If a font isn't installed on your computer, the text is burned in with a font of the same family, or with Gothic if none is available. Fonts without a bold style (such as MS Gothic) are rendered at normal weight
Color, outline, and opacity
Text color (default: white), outline width (0 to 25% of the text height, default 8%) and color (default: black), and opacity (default 100%)

Text lane

Above the clip lane of the timeline, a bar showing the display range appears for each text item, one row per item (text lane). Drag a bar to move it together with its display range, or drag either end to change the start or end. Selecting a bar also selects the same text in the text panel. Right-click to duplicate, copy, paste, or delete.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Watermark" %}

Use "Watermark" in the sidebar to overlay one image, such as a logo, on the entire exported video. Import the image in the media panel first (you can also set it with "Set as watermark" on an image in the media panel).

Image
Choose one of the imported images. "None" removes the watermark
Position
Choose from 9 positions from top left to bottom right (default: bottom right)
Size
Specify the image width as a percentage of the video width (1 to 100%, default 15%). The aspect ratio is kept
Opacity
0 to 100% (default 80%)
Horizontal margin / Vertical margin
Specify the distance from the edge of the video as a percentage of the video width / height (0 to 50%, default 2%)

To add a text watermark, use text shown over the entire video.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Audio and background music" %}

Use "Audio" in the sidebar to adjust the audio of the original video and audio tracks such as background music. On export, the audio of the original video and all audio tracks are layered (mixed) into the output.

Audio of the detected video
Volume of the original video (-40 to +12dB) and mute
Adding audio tracks
Use "Add to timeline" on audio media in the media panel (the track is placed at the playhead), or drag audio media onto the audio lane. If you place video media on the audio lane, you can use just the audio of that video
Volume / Mute
-40 to +12dB for each track. Mute temporarily silences just that track
Fade in / Fade out
Fades the start and end of each track over 0 to 20 seconds

Audio lane and waveform

Below the clip lane of the timeline, a bar appears for each audio track, one row per track (audio lane). The bar shows the waveform of the background music, so you can line things up while seeing where the music builds up or goes silent. Drag a bar to move it, or drag either end to trim the part that is used (the start and end of the song). Background music keeps playing across gaps between clips. For audio whose length is unknown, "length unknown" is shown instead of a bar.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Fade" %}

Fade the exported video in from black and out to black. There are two kinds: "Overall fade", applied to the start and end of the whole video, and "Clip fade", applied to the start and end of each clip. For the overall fade, enter the seconds in "Fade" in the sidebar. For clip fades, enter them in the section for the selected join, which opens when you choose "Fade" from the scissors at a join, or in the "Selected clip" section, which appears when you select a clip. You enter the seconds only in the sidebar (not on the timeline).

Choosing from the scissors
Press the {% include icon.html name="Scissors" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} badge (scissors) at a join between clips (or select it and press Enter / Space) to open a menu with 2 items, "Fade" and "Transition". Use ↑ / ↓ to move between the items, and press Esc or click outside the menu to close it. Choosing "Fade" opens "Fade" in the sidebar with that join in focus ("Transition" is covered in Transition)
Overall fade
In "Fade" in the sidebar, enter the fade-in and fade-out seconds for the whole video. Once you enter them, a ramp (triangle mark) of that length appears at the ends of the ruler
Clip fade
In the section for the selected join, enter the fade out of the clip before the join and the fade in of the clip after it. For the start of the first clip or the end of the last clip in the video, select the clip and enter the value in the "Selected clip" section. Once entered, a ramp appears at the bottom edge of the bar. Only the video of that clip fades (for clips of the original video, the audio fades too), and the watermark and text don't darken
Adjusting the seconds
Specify the length in seconds (0 to 20 seconds; 0 seconds means no fade). The actual number of frames that fade is shown next to the seconds. Clicking a ramp (or selecting it and pressing Enter) also opens "Fade" in the sidebar, and if you open it from a clip's ramp, that clip is selected. "Clear" removes both fades of the selected clip or join
Where fades can't be added
You can't add a clip fade to a clip end on the side of a join that has a transition. The input field in the sidebar is disabled, and the reason is shown. Remove the transition to set the fade
Go to start / Go to end
The buttons in "Fade" in the sidebar move the playhead to the start / end of the video. The overall fade applies only at both ends, so use these to check how it looks in the preview

Fades appear in the preview just as they do on export. Frames where the overall fade and a clip fade overlap get both. Set fades for each audio track in Audio and background music, and the switching at joins between clips in Transition.

{% include docs-h2.html kicker="Timeline editing" title="Transition" %}

Add a switching effect (transition) at the join between adjacent clips. Press the scissors at a join and choose "Transition" to open "Transition" in the sidebar (the drawer heading is also "Transition") with that join in focus, then choose the type and length. You enter the type and length only in the sidebar (not on the timeline). When there is only one clip, there is no join, so you can't add one. You can't add a clip fade to a clip end on the side of a join that has a transition.

Type
8 types: Fade / Dissolve / Wipe (left, right) / Slide (left, right) / Fade to black / Fade to white
Adding a transition
Press the {% include icon.html name="Scissors" class="inline h-3.5 w-3.5 align-[-2px]" %} badge (scissors) at a join on the timeline and choose "Transition" from the menu. The row for that join is selected in the sidebar's list of joins (each row shows the previous clip and the next clip). Choose a type ("None" removes it) and set the length from 0.1 to 5 seconds. Once added, a bar with diagonal lines appears at the join, with the same length as the overlap of the clips before and after it. Because the clips before and after overlap by that length, the whole video becomes that much shorter
Bar and badge
Clicking the bar with diagonal lines (or selecting it and pressing Enter) opens "Transition" in the sidebar directly, without the menu. Hover over the badge to see the current type and seconds
Joins with a gap
You can't add a transition at a join with a gap (black screen) in between. The reason is shown in that join's row in the sidebar (gaps aren't closed automatically). If you create a gap there after setting a transition, the transition stops taking effect, and it works again when you close the gap
Clip fade
At a join that has clip fades, the row shows the note "If you add a transition, the fades at this join are ignored on export." The fades aren't removed automatically
Preview
During the overlap, the clips before and after are combined and shown with the same kind of switching as on export. On the later clip's side, the preview doesn't show mosaic (it is applied on export)
